# Cap and pile design

The existing `Pier_Cap_Design_Optimizer.ipynb` now presents one **Cap and pile design explorer** for pier and end-bent caps supported directly on a single pile row. The cap-type selector labels the saved case. It does not generate end-bent loads or change the sectional calculation. Cap import retains its existing restrictions: uniform rectangular cap, straight horizontal global-X alignment, equally spaced round/square piles, and matching static FBMP 6.1.0 English-unit results. Backwalls, wingwalls, earth-pressure load generation, column/footing caps and strut-and-tie design remain outside the calculation.

## Workflow

1. **Upload FBMP XML** once. Pile results load immediately above the reinforcement controls. Review the cap preview, then click **Apply XML inputs** to update cap geometry and forces. Reapplying the same file preserves pile selections and trial inputs. A collapsed **Optional separate pile analysis** control accepts a different foundation run; it is not a second required upload. Source messages distinguish matching and different files, including changed cap geometry.
2. Review the pile-head matrix and governing compression, uplift, any-depth compression, model D/C and lateral displacement by exact limit state. The export preserves every tied governor and its concurrent force record.
3. Select a combination and check any set of piles to overlay displacement, moment magnitude, axial, model D/C and elastic stress profiles. All piles start selected. **All piles**, **Clear**, and **Highest model D/C** provide shortcuts. Each pile retains its color across every plot; solid/dashed lines distinguish components. Circles mark the first signed displacement crossing and orange diamonds mark the second; hover for exact station/elevation, direction, combination and bounding nodes. The readout identifies the deepest second crossing across **all** imported piles, combinations and DX/DY components, independent of display selection. When datums are entered, the displacement plot also marks the adopted minimum tip and names its controlling criterion. Selections persist in saved reviews and the HTML export. Reported FBMP material stress extrema remain separate and identify the actual governing pile. Enter a cutoff elevation to plot project elevation instead of distance along the pile.
4. Review the analyzed section, exported area/inertias, prestress and optional confirmed manual elastic properties. Model D/C and material stresses remain unchanged by an elastic-property override.
5. Open **Minimum tip**, enter **Ground EL (ft)** and **Pile cutoff EL (ft)** side by side at the top of the tab, then paste the five trial columns or load a CSV/TSV, with or without the workbook's **Trial / Load Comb / Pile / Embedment (ft) / Displacement (in)** headers. Click **Calculate minimum tip**. The comparison shows the trial criterion and the deepest second-zero criterion side by side, with critical depth below ground, added embedment, required embedment, tip elevation, controlling criterion and governing source. The deeper required tip controls; ties are named. The trial calculation still selects the shallowest trial whose displacement change to the next shallower trial is **≤ 0.1 in**, matching the spreadsheet. Its default is **required embedment = trial Lcrit + 5 ft**. **Add selected allowance below second crossing** is initially checked and applies that same allowance to the crossing criterion; uncheck it to compare the crossing elevation directly. This is an explicit comparison setting, not a claim that a 5-ft addition below the second crossing is independently prescribed. Lesser-of and percentage methods remain available and saved. Changes to ground, cutoff, allowance, zero band or method recalculate both the comparison and displacement plot immediately; editing the trial table requires Calculate again. With only profiles or trials available, the available candidate is shown as **comparison incomplete**. Active profiles with fewer than two crossings are listed, never assigned a zero depth or treated as a completed comparison. The unchanged trial calculation and its equations appear below the overall comparison.
6. Open **Geotech handoff** for a short report: **pile section information, calculated minimum tip elevation, and maximum factored pile-head compression/uplift in short tons**, with governing piles and combinations. Optional supplied weight, pipe diameter, toe condition and notes appear only when provided. **Update selected results** recalculates the current trial inputs; **Download geotech handoff** recalculates them again and downloads a ZIP containing a printable HTML report and small CSV tables. Missing qualifying trials or ground/scour elevation produces a message beside the button. The full **Download pile review** remains available for raw results and calculation details; it also contains the short handoff. The ordinary cap export includes a separate pile-review folder. Keep both cap and pile JSON files when saving both workflows. Rerunning the workbench cell preserves the loaded pile review and its controls. Older saved reviews also recalculate Lcrit from their trials instead of reusing a formerly typed critical depth.

The stress plot now labels **D/C = 1**, **±Fy for steel**, and **zero/decompression plus the XML concrete tensile peak for concrete**. Plot ranges include those limits even when all demands are smaller. The trial plots label the selected displacement-change tolerance and D/C = 1. Displacement, moment and axial profiles have no invented capacities or allowable values.

Immediately below the stress graph, **Cracking screen** compares each selected pile's gross-section elastic tensile stress with the model tensile peak. A separate **FBMP concrete cracking-strain check** compares the reported maximum concrete strain over all piles in the selected combination with the strain at the curve's first tensile peak. This detects a strain beyond that peak even when the corresponding stress has softened. It describes the selected run's reported state, not prior cracking history or an independent code serviceability check. Code service stress allowables are not supplied by the XML and are not evaluated. Older saved reviews without strain records request a fresh XML upload for that comparison.

## Calculation and data basis

- Required XML records: `PILE_GEOMETRY`, `PILE_GROUP/PILE_COORDINATES`, `PILE_DISPLACEMENTS`, `PILE_INTERNAL_FORCES`, load-combination definitions and complete static result records. One homogeneous section and one pile group are supported. Missing/duplicate stations, incorrect units, incomplete combinations and invalid connectivity stop import. XML entities are disabled.
- All original I/J force components remain in CSV. Axial tension is positive at I; J axial is reversed for a consistent section-force sign. Both ends stay separate at shared stations. Pile heads are the first I ends, not whichever station has maximum compression.
- Pile station distance follows the exported coordinates. FBMP Z increases downward in the reviewed export. Project elevation is `cutoff elevation − (Z − Z_head)/12`; no model Z coordinate is silently treated as a surveyed elevation.
- Elastic mean stress is `(P − Pp)/A`, in ksi. Rectangular/H symmetric-section bending uses `12 (|M2|/S2 + |M3|/S3)`. Circular bending uses `12 hypot(M2/S2, M3/S3)`. The maximum/minimum are mean ± bending. This reproduces the reference concrete method and circular-pipe biaxial method. Exported A and I are used because printed geometry can be rounded.
- Rectangular bar groups use FBMP's starting coordinates and orientation (2 or 3) to expand uniformly spaced bars to the opposite corner. Symmetric prestressed groups support automatic elastic stress plots and strand drawings. The allocation convention is documented in [FB-MultiPier's manual, Full Cross Section / Custom Allocation Methods](https://studylib.net/doc/28088488/fb-multipier). Eccentric prestress, unknown group arrangements, rotated, composite and unreviewed sections still suppress the automatic homogeneous elastic screen, with an explanation on the graph. An explicit manual-property control provides a confirmed symmetric, concentric approximation; it does not run a composite/nonlinear section analysis.
- FBMP's model D/C is distinct from elastic stress/Fy. Pile 0 stress records identify absent materials and are omitted. The XML material extrema do not provide the OUT cracking-warning text or convergence report; those are labeled unavailable, not assumed to be NO or converged. No independent AASHTO pile-capacity or buckling check is added.
- Concrete cracking thresholds use the actual exported stress–strain curve, including its strain coordinates, rather than a hardcoded modulus-of-rupture equation. The model tensile peak is not automatically a code service tension allowable. FBMP's configurable tensile curves and tensile peak are described in the [BSI Fall 2023 newsletter, page 14](https://bsi.ce.ufl.edu/newsletter/BSI-Newsletter-Fall-2023.pdf).
- Geotech load envelopes use pile **head** forces from strength and extreme-event combinations only; service and fatigue combinations are excluded. Compression and uplift are separate envelopes, converted using **1 short ton = 2 kip**, and every tied governing pile/combination is retained. These are design demands, not nominal geotechnical resistances. Section/load and second-crossing provenance come from the loaded XML; trial provenance comes from the separately named trial study. The handoff uses the governing minimum-tip comparison, including incomplete-comparison labels. Trial CSVs do not contain a replacement axial-load envelope. No selected-trial response is interpolated when the governing critical embedment is absent from a series.
- A solved XML does not contain the shortened-pile trial sequence in the supplied references. The trial table remains separate. It accepts optional `series, dc, dx_in, dy_in, converged, iterations, tolerance_kip`; changing governing pile/combination across envelope trials is allowed. Separate independently analyzed series must be labeled. Missing convergence or D/C stays visible.
- Trials are sorted by embedment, so reversing pasted row order has no effect. Duplicate embedments within a series are rejected. For each trial, Δ is the absolute displacement difference to the next shallower trial. Lcrit is the smallest embedment with Δ ≤ the tolerance, matching **Min Tip Paste G6 = MIN(H15:H114)** and its F/G/H row formulas. This includes any qualifying pair rather than imposing a separate continuous-sequence rule. If multiple series are supplied, each needs a qualifying pair and the largest series Lcrit governs. Supplied D/C or convergence failures are reported separately and do not change the spreadsheet's displacement-only selection formula. Missing D/C or convergence remains unavailable. The inclusive comparison allows a 1e-12-in numerical tolerance at the threshold. Variable trial spacing is flagged.
- The default extension is a fixed `5 ft`, so trial required embedment is `Lcrit + 5 ft`, including when Lcrit is less than 25 ft. The former worksheet default `min(5 ft, 0.20 × Lcrit)` from **Min Tip Paste G10** and **PP30_BB217R D28** remains an explicit shaft/reference option. Inputs remain editable and the selected method is retained in saved reviews. Trial depth/addition can calculate without ground, but converting the profile crossing into embedment below ground requires both cutoff and ground/scour elevations. Neither datum is inferred from arbitrary model coordinates.
- Second crossings use **signed DX and DY separately**, never the nonnegative lateral resultant. Stations are ordered from head to tip; adjacent opposite signs are linearly interpolated in both along-pile and vertical coordinates. The second crossing in each profile is used, even if additional deeper oscillations exist. The deepest of these second crossings by **vertical depth** governs across all imported piles and combinations, including service. The zero band defaults to `±0.000001 in` and is editable. A zero/near-zero node counts only when bracketed by opposite signs; multiple zero-band nodes count once at their deeper end and are labeled accordingly. Touches, zero heads and zero tails do not add crossings; wholly inactive components are labeled within the zero band. No crossing is extrapolated beyond the model. All tied governors are retained. The [FHWA GEC 9 discussion of second-zero-deflection fixity](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/engineering/geotech/pubs/hif18031.pdf) describes the concept in a factored-load P–Δ context; this requested all-profile comparison reviews imported results and does not independently verify the model's P–Δ settings or establish all minimum-penetration requirements.
- Tip elevation uses the ground/scour datum minus required embedment; total length also uses the separate cutoff datum. Candidates are compared before rounding. Whole-foot tip rounding uses mathematical floor, including negative elevations. Length uses ceiling to the adopted rounded tip, keeping both reported quantities compatible when cutoff elevation has a fractional foot. This can add a foot compared with independently rounding length before tip. It also avoids Excel ROUNDDOWN's toward-zero behavior for negative tips.

Pile review format 4 saves the zero band and crossing-allowance setting; older reviews remain loadable and are recalculated. The full export includes `pile_zero_crossings.csv`, `minimum_tip_criteria.csv` and the combined `minimum_tip_result.json`, plus the marked displacement plot and comparison in HTML. The geotech handoff and its selection CSV use that same controlling result.

The January 2026 [FDOT Structures Manual](https://www.fdot.gov/structures/structuresmanual/currentrelease/structuresmanual.shtm), **SDG 3.5.9.B.3–4, printed pp. 3-16–3-17**, calls for 5-ft trial intervals and, for driven piles, the greater of **Lcrit + 5 ft** and the penetration satisfying LRFD 10.7.6, together with Service-limit deflection checks. **SDG 3.6.5.B.4, printed p. 3-29**, retains the lesser of 5 ft and 20% for drilled shafts. The new default implements the driven-pile 5-ft addition; the notebook does not independently establish the greater LRFD-required penetration, uplift/settlement tip or Service deflection acceptance. Those checks and the governing project edition remain separate. Existing trial selection follows the reference adjacent-pair algorithm described above and does not enforce 5-ft intervals. [BSI's product description](https://bsi.ce.ufl.edu/products/default.aspx/1000) describes the nonlinear analysis performed by FB-MultiPier; this notebook reviews exported results and does not replace that solver.

## Verification

Regression tests compare all 1,088 element-end records and 560 displacement records in the reviewed XML fixture against the source, check units/connectivity/missing data, retain uplift, and separate reported stresses from elastic calculations. Sixty concrete stress stations reproduce the supplied workbook's cached results. The 25 reference trials automatically produce Lcrit = 32.74 ft, added embedment = 5 ft, and the unrounded −13.24-ft tip from reference elevation 24.5 ft. No manual selection is supplied. These reference calculations are separate from the steel XML run. An independent 20-ft Lcrit case verifies that the default adds 5 ft rather than 4 ft. Checks cover missing ground with a calculated addition, automatic datum/allowance updates, zero/negative elevations, whole-foot rounding, the inclusive 0.1-in boundary, all extension methods, missing qualifying pairs, governing series, saved-method preservation and automatic handoff downloads.

Crossing tests cover interpolation, exact-zero nodes, zero intervals, touches, near-zero tails, missing crossings, multiple crossings, tied criteria, both governing criteria, batter/datum conversion, rounding, live edits, selection independence and JSON/export consistency. With the selected 5-ft crossing allowance, its −30.012631-ft candidate controls over the trial-only −13.24-ft candidate. These deliberately combined fixtures test comparison mechanics, not a common project design.
